## Why

A relay session moves bytes. Every component that uses one therefore builds and encodes its own NIP-01 envelope, and then hand-parses every reply on a connection it shares with the others. Both halves of the protocol leak outward, and both go wrong.

**Outbound.** Three crates construct envelopes and call `fava_wire::encode_client` themselves: `fava-observe` builds `REQ` and `CLOSE` (`crates/fava-observe/src/operations.rs:134`, `:167`, `:209`), `fava-publisher-nip01` builds `EVENT` and `AUTH` (`crates/fava-publisher-nip01/src/lib.rs:91`, `:184`, `:289`), and `fava-auth` builds `AUTH` (`crates/fava-auth/src/authenticator/answer.rs:173`). Each then wraps the bytes in a caller-minted `HandoffCorrelation` — `fava-observe` runs a `1_u64..` counter (`operations.rs:132`), `fava-auth` synthesizes one from the session generation (`answer.rs:186`), `fava-publisher-nip01` reuses the attempt number (`lib.rs:103`). No production code ever reads it back: every caller matches on the `HandoffOutcome` variant and discards the correlation the outcome carries. `send` is `async` and returns that one frame's outcome, so the await already correlates. The only readers are `fava-transport-testkit`'s own conformance assertion (`crates/fava-transport-testkit/src/lib.rs:156`) and its fake's stranded-frame bookkeeping.

**Inbound.** `fava-transport` copies raw bytes to every consumer of a session. `fava-observe`, `fava-publisher-nip01`, and `fava-auth` each take a stream, decode every frame, and scan for what they own. Correlation is right in principle — each wire key has one owner — but it is done once per consumer, and the publisher's copy treats other components' messages as its own answer. `Nip01Publisher` (`crates/fava-publisher-nip01/src/lib.rs:319-362`) fails three ways from that one root:

- it gives up after 64 frames, reporting `matching OK absent after 64 relay frames` as an unknown outcome for an event the relay accepted (`:362`), because the window counts frames nobody addressed to it;
- a relay `NOTICE`, which NIP-01 correlates to nothing, ends the attempt as an unknown outcome (`:349`);
- an unsolicited `AUTH` challenge — which `fava-auth` owns and answers (`crates/fava-auth/src/authenticator/answer.rs:173`) — ends the attempt as `AuthenticationRequired` (`:346`) whether or not the session is already authenticated.

A frame that fails to decode does the same, ending an unrelated in-flight publish (`:326`).

The correct demultiplexer already exists one crate over. `fava-observe`'s `accept` (`crates/fava-observe/src/ingest.rs:43`) resolves every subscription-correlated message against the set installed on the current generation and returns `Unattributed` rather than acting. Its table is `Slot.installed` (`crates/fava-observe/src/slot.rs:26`), invalidated by `Slot::advance` (`:65`) on every generation change. It is private to `fava-observe`, so the publisher grew a frame counter instead.

The two halves are one thing. A session that constructs the `REQ` knows the subscription id; a session that constructs the `EVENT` knows the event id. The correlation the inbound side needs is a by-product of the outbound verb, not a second mechanism to register and keep in sync.

The boundary that forbids this buys nothing. `ARCHITECTURE.md:408` gives protocol meaning to `fava-wire` and bytes to `fava-transport`; `ARCHITECTURE.md:1777` restricts `fava-transport-websocket` to `fava-wire` "only for optional framing diagnostics". NIP-01 is not an optional dialect layered over a neutral relay socket — it is the only thing a Nostr relay speaks. Every consumer of `RelaySession` already depends on `fava-wire`, and `fava-wire` is a leaf crate with no fava dependencies, so the boundary separates two layers that both already speak the protocol. It costs three hand-written envelope builders, three hand-written demultiplexers, one decode per consumer per frame, and a correlation token nobody reads.

## What Changes

- **BREAKING** `RelaySession` gains one verb per client message Fava sends. `req(filters)` opens a subscription, `event(signed)` publishes, `auth(signed)` answers a challenge. Each takes protocol values and owns the envelope, the encoding, and the frame bound. No crate outside the transport constructs a `ClientMessage` or calls `encode_client` to reach a relay.
- **BREAKING** Remove `RelaySession::send`. A byte-level escape hatch would reintroduce exactly the leak the verbs close.
- **BREAKING** Remove `HandoffCorrelation` and the `correlation` field from all three `HandoffOutcome` variants. Each verb returns its own handoff outcome to its own awaiter, so the await is the correlation.
- **BREAKING** `fava-transport` takes a dependency on `fava-wire` and decodes each inbound frame exactly once, in the socket reader.
- **BREAKING** Each verb returns a handle for what comes back. `req` yields a subscription delivering exactly that subscription's `EVENT`, `EOSE`, `CLOSED`, `COUNT`, `NEG-MSG`, and `NEG-ERR`; `event` and `auth` yield an acknowledgement delivering exactly that event's `OK`.
- **BREAKING** Each handle delivers its own narrow type, not a shared inbound enum. A subscription yields an event, end-of-stored-events, a closure, bounded loss, or its generation ending. An acknowledgement settles as accepted, rejected, or its generation ending, and that ending names whether the connection dropped or the reconnect budget ran out, because a publication attempt must report which.
- **BREAKING** Remove `RelaySession::messages()`, `RelayInbound`, and `RelayMessageStream`. With challenges on their own accessor and lifecycle carried by every handle, nothing is left for a session-wide stream but leftovers, which are diagnostics rather than a stream anyone consumes.
- Add `RelaySession::auth_challenges()`. Exactly one component reads relay challenges and none sends anything to correlate them to, so they get a named accessor rather than a claim or a filtered firehose.
- A message no handle claims — a `NOTICE`, an `EVENT` naming an unknown subscription, an `OK` naming an unknown event — and bytes that do not decode are counted through `fava-diagnostics` with a bounded reason. They never end work that did not ask for them.
- **BREAKING** The session mints the subscription identifier. `req` takes filters and reports the identifier it chose on the handle; callers stop supplying one. Identifiers are opaque and fixed-width — a namespace prefix and a counter — inside NIP-01's guaranteed 64 characters, so a clash is impossible by construction and the exact encoded length of a `REQ` is knowable before the identifier exists. Nothing is lost by dropping the planner's `<revision>-<ordinal>` text: nothing parses it, and its authority number restarts each run (`crates/fava-subscriptions/src/plan.rs:56`), so it never identified anything outside the live process.
- No exclusivity rule and no duplicate refusal. Subscription identifiers cannot collide because the session chooses them, and an acknowledgement fans out: two callers publishing the same event each get a handle and each sees the relay's `OK`.
- Closing a subscription lives on its handle. `close()` sends the closure and awaits its handoff outcome; dropping the handle enqueues the same closure and returns without waiting, so a subscription cannot stop being read while the relay keeps sending it. A handle dropped after its generation advanced enqueues nothing, because the relay already forgot the subscription.
- **BREAKING** Delete `WithdrawalReason` and collapse `SubscriptionPlan::close` to `Vec<SubscriptionId>`. No production planner ever constructs `WithdrawalReason::Regrouped` — both planners' `withdrawals()` emit only `ConstraintChanged` or `DemandWithdrawn` (`crates/fava-subscriptions-standard/src/diff.rs:104`, `crates/fava-subscriptions-no-grouping/src/lib.rs:307`), and neither of those is ever branched on; they are only `Debug`-printed into a diagnostic at `crates/fava-observe/src/facts.rs:95`. The executor's open-before-close guard (`crates/fava-observe/src/operations.rs:160`) and the conformance rule that protects it therefore guard a case that cannot arise. Deleting them also removes the only plan field that names a subscription before it is opened, which is what made session-minted identifiers cheap. The executor closes a withdrawn subscription once nothing it served is still demanded and uncovered — the same guarantee, derived rather than declared. The two hand-built plans exercising `Regrouped` (`crates/fava-subscriptions/tests/running.rs:81`, `:191`) retire with it.
- **BREAKING** `PlannedSubscription` drops its `id` and absorbs `EoseCompleteness`, so a planned subscription is its own attribution. `SubscriptionAttribution` covers the retained set only, where wire identifiers exist. This deletes the duplication that conformance rules C5 and C7 exist to cross-check — `PlannedSubscription` and `AttributedSubscription` carry the same filters and demand today — along with `PlanConformanceError::UnknownSuccessor`, `SubscriptionPlanError::DuplicateSubscription`, and the C2 and C3 collision rules, which a session-minted identifier makes unreachable.
- **BREAKING** Delete `wire::mint` from `fava-subscriptions-standard` (`crates/fava-subscriptions-standard/src/wire.rs:32`) and its counterpart in `fava-subscriptions-no-grouping` (`crates/fava-subscriptions-no-grouping/src/lib.rs:95`), along with `ShortfallReason::SubscriptionIdTooLong` and the two planner checks that raise it (`crates/fava-subscriptions-standard/src/lib.rs:277`, `crates/fava-subscriptions-no-grouping/src/lib.rs:224`). A fixed-width identifier inside NIP-01's guaranteed 64 cannot be too long, so the shortfall is unreachable — as it already is in production, where `nip11_fetch` attempts only `ws://` and every `wss://` relay's declared limit stays unknown (`crates/fava-observe/src/operations.rs:308`).
- A new connection generation ends every live handle, because the wire state they name did not survive the connection. Consumers already replay demand on reconnect; an ended handle reports that rather than hanging.
- Delete `Nip01Publisher`'s frame-scanning loop, its `MAX_INBOUND_FRAMES` bound, and its `NOTICE`, `AUTH`, and decode-error branches. An attempt awaits its own acknowledgement, bounded by its own deadline and by session liveness.
- Delete the frame decoding in `fava_observe::ingest::accept`, in `fava_auth::authenticator::session_watch::admit`, and in both `fava-publisher-nip01` publish paths. Attribution of a subscription to the observations that own it stays in `fava-observe`; only the UTF-8 and JSON handling below it goes.
- **BREAKING** Replace `fava-transport-testkit`'s `require_inbound_fan_out` with verb conformance, and rework its fake to answer verbs rather than bytes.
- Amend `docs/spec/ARCHITECTURE.md` where it assigns bytes to the transport boundary: the responsibility line (`:76`), the `fava-wire` relationship (`:408`), the `fava-transport` contract, owned state, and `HandoffCorrelation` paragraph (`:1696-1757`), the `fava-transport-websocket` framing-diagnostics restriction (`:1777`), the inbound flow diagram (`:2946-2948`), and the replaceable-boundary table rows (`:3353`, `:3850`).

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `transport/session-protocol`: what a relay session accepts from the components using it — protocol values, not envelopes — what each verb returns, and what happens when a verb names a wire key that is already held.
- `transport/message-routing`: what a relay session delivers and to whom, what happens to a message no component claims or to bytes that do not decode, and what a reconnect does to claimed keys.
- `publication/attempt-acknowledgement`: how one publication attempt learns its own outcome on a connection it shares with queries and other attempts, and what bounds that wait.

### Modified Capabilities

None. `openspec/specs/` holds no main specs yet, so all three capabilities are introduced here.

## Impact

Supersedes and replaces the `route-relay-messages` change, which covered the inbound half only and specified an explicit claim registered separately from the frames that create a subscription. The verbs make that mechanism unnecessary: the claim is a by-product of the `REQ` or `EVENT` the session itself builds. That change is deleted and its evidence carried here.

`fava-subscriptions` changes shape: the planner proposes subscriptions without naming them, and the executor names them by sending. Behaviour is unchanged — the standard planner already refuses to rewrite a running subscription (`crates/fava-subscriptions-standard/src/diff.rs:84`, `crates/fava-observe/src/admission.rs`), so nothing that fires today stops firing. The 10ms first-arrival-anchored admission cohort and `filter_covers` attachment to a live subscription are untouched.

`fava-transport` gains `fava-wire`, a leaf with no fava dependencies, so no cycle is introduced. `fava-transport-websocket` encodes in the verb and decodes in `driver::admit_frame` (`crates/fava-transport-websocket/src/driver.rs:258`), immediately after the existing `max_frame_bytes` check; `fanout.rs` becomes a router.

Removed public API: `RelaySession::send`, `RelaySession::messages`, `HandoffCorrelation`, the `correlation` field on every `HandoffOutcome` variant, `RelayInbound`, `RelayMessageStream`, `WithdrawalReason`, `WithdrawnSubscription`, `PlannedSubscription::id`, `ShortfallReason::SubscriptionIdTooLong`, `SubscriptionPlanError::DuplicateSubscription`, `PlanConformanceError::{UnknownSuccessor, ReopenedInstalled, OverlappingBuckets}`, `require_inbound_fan_out`.

New public API: `RelaySession::req`, `event`, `auth`, `auth_challenges` and the handles they return, with one narrow item type per handle.

Kept, adapted: `fava-subscriptions-standard` and `fava-subscriptions-no-grouping` still measure a `REQ`'s exact encoded byte length against the relay's advertised message limit (`crates/fava-subscriptions-standard/src/wire.rs:46`, `crates/fava-subscriptions-no-grouping/src/lib.rs:255`). That happens during planning, with no session in hand, and sends nothing. Today it measures with a probe identifier the planner mints (`crates/fava-subscriptions-standard/src/lib.rs:145`); it will measure with the fixed identifier width the session declares, which keeps the measurement exact rather than approximate.

Changed behavior: a publication attempt no longer reports an unknown outcome or `AuthenticationRequired` because of traffic belonging to another component, and is bounded by its deadline rather than by a frame count.

Ten `impl Transport` sites outside the standard implementation are affected — `fava-transport-testkit`'s fake, `fava-publication`'s harness, and eight test doubles under `crates/fava/tests/` and `crates/fava-write-store-redb/tests/`. Most get shorter: a fake answers `req` and `event` instead of parsing JSON its author wrote by hand.

Constrained by `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md` OWN-02: `fava-observe` exclusively owns retained logical demand and the desired wire-subscription plan, and the transport "executes it and owns none of it". The session holds no filters beyond the life of the `REQ` frame it encodes, no retained demand, no plan revision, and no observation identity. Minting a subscription identifier is naming one connection's wire state, not owning the plan: the planner still decides which subscriptions exist and what each carries, and `fava-observe` still keys its own table by the identifier the session returns. OWN-03 is untouched: establishment, generation, reconnect, backoff, and close stay where they are.

Does not claim `Nip42Publisher`'s deletion or `fava-observe`'s `RelayMessage::Auth` branch (`crates/fava-observe/src/ingest.rs:114`); both belong to the in-flight `own-relay-authentication` change. Sequence after it or rebase onto it.
