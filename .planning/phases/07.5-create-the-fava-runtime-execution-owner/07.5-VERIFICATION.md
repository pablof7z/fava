## SC1 - The execution owner exists and owns its resources: PASS WITH GAPS

### Verified

**Join registry.** `crates/fava-runtime/src/runtime.rs:52-57` holds
`registry: Mutex<BTreeMap<u64, Registered>>` where `Registered {name: TaskName, handle: JoinHandle}`.
`register` (`:321-340`) holds the registry lock across the spawn so a task cannot deregister itself
before it is registered - a real concurrency detail, correctly reasoned about in the comment. The
owner's `TaskHandle` and the registry's `JoinHandle` are separate grips, so shutdown can join work
whose handle was dropped. Proved by `tests/task_lifecycle.rs:64
a_task_whose_handle_was_dropped_is_still_joined` and `:161 a_completed_task_frees_its_registry_slot`.
`outstanding_tasks()` (`:157`) exposes the registry for falsification.

**Bounded channels with typed refusal.** `Runtime::channel` (`:178`) and `default_channel` (`:184`).
A full channel refuses and hands the item back rather than parking:
`tests/bounded_channel.rs:16 a_full_command_channel_refuses_and_returns_the_item`,
`:48 a_full_channel_refuses_instead_of_parking`,
`:67 send_before_waits_for_capacity_then_gives_up_with_the_item`. A channel created after shutdown
begins is created already closed (`:180`, proved at `tests/bounded_channel.rs:167`).

**Provider-operation identity.** `ProviderCompletion<T>` (`src/provider.rs:20-63`) has five variants
- `Completed`, `TimedOut{after}`, `Panicked{detail}`, `Cancelled`, `Refused` - and *every* one
carries both `operation: OperationName` and `generation: OperationGeneration`. Total accessors
`generation()` and `operation()` at `:67,:81`. `tests/provider_isolation.rs:299
every_completion_variant_carries_its_authorising_identity` pins this, and `:269
a_stale_completion_is_rejectable_by_generation` proves the owner can reject a superseded one.
Panic payloads are bounded to 512 bytes on a char boundary (`provider.rs:13,:112-124`).

**Cancellation propagation.** `src/cancel.rs` with `root()`/`child()`. Six behavioural tests:
`tests/task_lifecycle.rs:277 cancellation_propagates_from_an_owner_token_to_its_children`,
`:294 a_child_derived_after_cancellation_is_already_cancelled`,
`:302 cancelling_a_child_leaves_its_parent_and_siblings_running`,
`:315 propagation_does_not_depend_on_holding_intermediate_tokens`,
`:332 shutdown_fires_every_token_rooted_in_the_runtime`.

**Shutdown deadlines.** `shutdown(deadline)` (`:290-318`) sets the shutting-down flag, cancels the
root token, drains the registry, and joins each task against a single absolute `Instant` so the
deadline is for the whole set rather than per task. On expiry it aborts and returns
`RuntimeError::ShutdownIncomplete{tasks}` naming the exact offenders. Proved at
`tests/task_lifecycle.rs:25`, `:97 shutdown_names_the_exact_task_that_outlived_the_deadline`,
`:80 repeated_shutdown_is_harmless`, `:211 a_task_aborted_at_shutdown_reports_abortion_to_its_owner`.

**Detach-not-abort on timeout.** `call_provider` documents and implements the right thing at
`:196-200`: a timed-out or cancelled provider future is detached, not aborted, because "a provider
mid-write to its own store must not be torn apart", and it stays in the join registry so shutdown
either joins it or names it. Proved by
`tests/provider_isolation.rs:27 stalled_provider_yields_timed_out_completion_and_shutdown_still_joins`.

### Gaps

**The bounded channel has no consumer.** `fava_runtime::{Sender, Receiver, SendRefusal,
SendRefused}` are referenced nowhere outside `crates/fava-runtime`. The observation owner's own
command inbox is a raw `tokio::sync::mpsc::channel(REPORTS)` at
`crates/fava-observe/src/engine.rs:122`, which parks on full rather than refusing. So the criterion's
"bounded command and completion channels" is implemented, tested, configured
(`default_channel_depth: 1_024` at `crates/fava/src/builder.rs:254` and
`crates/fava-observe/src/observer.rs:344`) - and unused. The boundedness property it provides does
not hold anywhere in the shipped engine.

**Timers have no consumer.** `Runtime::sleep` (`:284`) is called nowhere.

**Dead refusal variant.** `RuntimeError::ProviderOperationLimit` (`:376`) is never constructed; the
provider-bound path reports `ProviderCompletion::Refused` instead. The doc comment explains the
intent, but no owner admits provider work through a fallible operation, so the variant is
unreachable.

**Vocabulary gate false positive against this crate.** `python3 tools/check_vocabulary.py` reports
`registered public symbol does not exist: fava_runtime::OperationGeneration`. It does exist -
`crates/fava-runtime/src/generation.rs:8` re-exports it from `fava-query` per FROZEN-CONTRACTS
section 0 - but the checker sees declarations only. Recorded here because it is the one diagnostic
this phase's crate contributes to the red vocabulary gate.

## SC2 - Every provider call under a Fava-owned deadline: FAIL

### The count

`Runtime::call_provider` has **three** call sites in the entire workspace, all in
`crates/fava-observe/src/operations.rs`, all for one contract:

| Operation slot | Provider | Deadline source |
|---|---|---|
| `transport.acquire_session` | `dyn Transport` | `request.deadlines.establish` |
| `transport.send` | `dyn Transport` | caller-supplied |
| `transport.close` | `dyn Transport` | caller-supplied |

The pattern there is correct and worth crediting: `operations.rs:47-52` spawns a cancellable
runtime task, calls `call_provider` inside it with the operation slot, the owner's generation, and
the deadline, and reports the `ProviderCompletion` back to the owner, which rejects it if the
generation has moved on. That is exactly what the architecture asks for.

Every other provider contract in the workspace is invoked without it.

| Provider contract | Call site | How it is called |
|---|---|---|
| `dyn Signer` | `crates/fava-publication/src/sign.rs:79` | bare `.await` inside a detached `tokio::spawn` |
| `dyn Publisher` | `crates/fava-publication/src/delivery.rs:183-186` | bare `.await`; deadline passed to the provider as data |
| `dyn Router` | `crates/fava-routing/src/chain.rs:118`, `:70` | synchronous, panic-isolated, no deadline |
| `dyn EventCache` | `crates/fava-observe/src/ingest.rs:73` (via `fava_ingest`) | synchronous, no deadline |
| `dyn WriteStore` | `crates/fava-publication/src/sign.rs:101`, `delivery.rs:187` | synchronous, no deadline |
| `dyn QueryEvaluator` | `crates/fava-observe/src/sources.rs` | synchronous, no deadline |
| `dyn SubscriptionPlanner` | `crates/fava-observe/src/engine.rs:352` | synchronous, no deadline |
| `dyn DeliveryPolicy` | `crates/fava-publication/src/delivery.rs` | synchronous, no deadline |

The runtime's own doc comment states the rule this violates, at
`crates/fava-runtime/src/runtime.rs:193-194`:

> This is the only sanctioned way to await a provider. A bare `.await` on a `dyn` provider anywhere
> in a lifecycle owner is a contract violation.

The signer case is the sharpest. `crates/fava-publication/src/sign.rs:78-79` spawns a detached task
and bare-awaits `signer.sign_event(unsigned, cancel)`. There is no `TimedOut`, so an
application-supplied signer that never resolves parks that publication permanently with no
attributable outcome. The stale-completion check that follows (`:81-95`) is real and good, but it
only runs if the provider eventually answers.

The publisher case is a deadline in name only. `delivery.rs:180` sets `timeout: ATTEMPT_TIMEOUT`
(5 seconds, `:14`) *inside the `PublishAttempt` struct handed to the provider*, then bare-awaits
`self.publisher.publish(...)` at `:185`. Enforcement is delegated to the provider. A substituted
publisher that ignores the field is unbounded - which directly falsifies the criterion's last
sentence, "A substituted provider is bounded by the same policy as the default one." It is bounded
by the same *request*, not the same *policy*.

### What is verified

Where `call_provider` is used, isolation is real and proved at the owner:
`tests/provider_isolation.rs:84 panicking_provider_becomes_a_typed_completion`,
`:125 a_panicking_provider_does_not_poison_the_registry_or_shutdown`,
`:143 a_blocked_provider_cannot_block_unrelated_owner_progress`,
`:179 a_provider_call_in_flight_at_shutdown_completes_as_cancelled`,
`:212 a_provider_call_after_shutdown_is_refused`,
`:231 provider_calls_beyond_the_declared_operation_bound_are_refused`. These are component-level
against the runtime, not against the assembled engine, which is consistent with OWN-05's own
"hardened at Phase 8" note.

## SC4 - Bounded reconnect policy: PASS WITH GAPS

### Verified

**Growth, ceiling, jitter.** `crates/fava-transport-websocket/src/backoff.rs`. `next_delay()`
(`:43-55`) doubles `base` (100ms, `:26`) by `1 << attempt.min(16)`, caps at `ceiling` (30s, `:28`),
and applies full jitter through a deterministic xorshift (`:64-69`) so "a fleet of sessions never
re-dials in lockstep". `reset()` (`:58`) forgets the growth after a generation survives. All four
properties the criterion names are present, in the transport, which is the owner the ledger assigns
(`ARCH:1588-1589`).

**Attempt bound and typed exhaustion.** `driver.rs:93-111 reconnect` loops `1..=budget` and on
exhaustion returns `Err((budget, last_reason))`, which `drive` (`:75-83`) converts into
`RelayInbound::ReconnectExhausted{identity, attempts, reason}`
(`crates/fava-transport/src/inbound.rs:40`) fanned out to every consumer, then closes the session.
The observation owner consumes it at `crates/fava-observe/src/completions.rs:196` and turns it into
`RelaySourceState::Unreachable{attempts, detail}`
(`crates/fava-query/src/evidence.rs:231-238`), which is on the public `QueryEvidence` surface.
The chain from budget exhaustion to an application-visible typed fact is complete.
Contract-level proof: `crates/fava-transport-websocket/tests/conformance.rs:395` and
`crates/fava-transport-testkit/tests/adversarial.rs:249` both assert on `ReconnectExhausted`.

**One reconnect lifecycle for N observations.** Structural: reconnect lives inside the session
driver, one per `RelaySessionKey`, and equivalent observations share one session. Proved through the
assembled public API at `crates/fava/tests/explicit_live.rs:59-93`: two observations of one query
yield `transport.dials(&key) == 1` and `holders == 1`, and the count stays 1 after the first closes.
One session means one driver means one backoff means one reconnect lifecycle. Also
`crates/fava-observe/tests/shared_work.rs:209 a_stalled_relay_never_delays_a_reachable_one`.

### Gaps

**The attempt bound is never applied in production.** Every `OpenRelaySession` the shipped
composition builds sets `reconnect_attempts: None`:

- `crates/fava-observe/src/engine.rs:290` - the observation owner's only relay acquisition
- `crates/fava-publisher-nip01/src/lib.rs:35`
- `apps/canary/src/grouping.rs:715`

`driver.rs:98-100` resolves `None` to `usize::MAX`. The only places a real budget is ever set are
test files (`fava-transport-websocket/tests/conformance.rs:49,222,342,385`,
`fava-transport-testkit/tests/adversarial.rs:40`, `tests/falsifiers.rs:38`). So the shipped engine
retries an unreachable relay forever, `ReconnectExhausted` is unreachable in production, and
`RelaySourceState::Unreachable` can never be produced by budget exhaustion on the real path. The
contract is bounded; the composition is not. `AGENTS.md:26` gate 5 requires "externally influenced
inputs, outputs, queues, observations, and retained evidence have explicit bounds or typed
refusal/shortfall" - an unbounded retry loop against a hostile or dead relay is the case that gate
names.

**A backoff test does not test what it is named for.**
`backoff.rs:76-82 delays_grow_and_never_exceed_the_ceiling` runs 40 iterations and asserts only
`<= CEILING`. Growth is never asserted, and cannot be asserted per-sample given full jitter. The
ceiling half is proved; the growth half is proved by nothing. `two_sessions_do_not_re_dial_in_lockstep`
(`:84-90`) and `a_surviving_generation_resets_the_growth` (`:93-100`) are both genuine and do
discriminate.

## Gaps Summary

The crate is real and it is used. `fava-runtime` is 989 lines of careful implementation - a join
registry that holds its lock across the spawn, a provider call that detaches rather than aborts on
timeout so a provider mid-write is not torn apart, five completion variants that all carry their
authorising generation, a shutdown that names the exact task that outlived its deadline - backed by
37 owner-level tests that all pass. `fava-observe` genuinely runs its engine, its transport
operations, its route session, and its query sources on it. The answer to "orphan crate with tests
and no callers" is no.

What did not happen is the second half of the phase: making it *the* execution owner rather than
*an* execution owner. It bounds one provider contract out of eight. `call_provider` has three call
sites, all Transport, all in one file. The signer is bare-awaited inside a detached task with no
timeout; the publisher is handed a duration and trusted to honour it, which is a provider-owned
deadline wearing a Fava-owned deadline's name; routers, caches, stores, evaluators, and planners
have no deadline at all. Nine detached `tokio::spawn` calls remain in production source, including
the WebSocket session driver that owns the socket and the reconnect loop, and the publication
custody loop. And `Runtime::shutdown` - the whole point of the join registry - is called by nothing
outside the crate's own tests, so even the eight registered tasks are never joined.

The reconnect policy has every property the criterion names and none of them is switched on: three
production call sites pass `reconnect_attempts: None`, the driver reads that as `usize::MAX`, and
the typed exhaustion shortfall that the whole chain exists to deliver cannot occur on the real path.

The unifying observation is the one the phase's own runtime doc states and the workspace ignores:
*"A bare `.await` on a `dyn` provider anywhere in a lifecycle owner is a contract violation."* The
sentence was written; the violations were not removed. Nothing detects them, because the falsifier
that would - `no_crate_outside_fava_runtime_spawns_a_task`, named by OWN-05 - is one of the fifteen
that 07.3 declared executable and never wrote.
